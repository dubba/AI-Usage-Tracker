Best for Seeing when you burn: weekdays, gaps, streaks, and a quarter at a glance. Understanding pace against the weekly limit, and how this week compares with past ones. Many accounts, page-level health, and scanning without scrolling through tall cards.
7 days Current bar chart, lightly refined. Reset line and stats stay. Current bar chart. The week is framed as one cycle. Stacked bars for the whole portfolio, plus 7-bar sparklines in each row.
30 days A real month grid: day tiles with numbers and values, and each week's total in the left gutter. Four to five burn-up curves, one per limit cycle, with an even-pace guide. Stacked 7-day-average area, a weekday rhythm chart, and expandable account rows.
90 days Thirteen-plus weekly columns of squares, with a weekly-burn bar under each: the quarter on one screen. Thirteen-week fill trend, then this week against a typical week. Ninety-day portfolio area; per-account detail opens in place.
Effort Low–medium. One new chart component; data and page shell unchanged. Medium. Cycle math from existing resets; two new chart components. High. New page structure, selection state, responsive table, three chart types.
Watch out Color-only encoding: needs tooltips, the screen-reader table, and a non-color cue for estimates. Cumulative curves need a legend; exact daily values are one hover away, not printed. Per-account detail moves behind a click; series colors add a second palette dimension.
My lean: start with A — it is the smallest structural change that makes 30 and 90 days feel purpose-built, and it keeps the 7-day bars untouched. If the account list is likely to grow past six, pair A's heat grid with C's summary band and compact rows. Nothing is implemented yet; I'll wait for your pick.
